# 007 T5 Lab: bands and budgets per pool against 2024-2026 data (18 Oct 2026)

Ticket otto/mamoru#151. Spec `specs/007-onchain-action-policy/spec.md` §Parameters, §Liveness cost, §Max loss.
Branch `lab/151-t5-bands` off T3 (`feat/151-t3-engine-v4`, fd56c21). Lab only: no engine, grant or contract
code changes; `MamoruActionPolicy` is still not wired into `decide` or session enforcement anywhere (that is T4's
precheck and T7's rollout). This unit adds a measurement module to `packages/backtest` (the Lab) and this report.

## Protocol

Not a TRAIN/TUNE/TEST search: spec 007 already proposes the bands (10/100/100 ticks), the budgets (`W_p = 1xCp`
per day, `T_p = 3xCp` per approval) and asks T5 to **confirm** them against history, so every runnable window of
`LAB_WINDOWS` (`packages/backtest/src/windows.ts`) is used, both splits, reported together. The three pools are
`pool:USDC/USDT/100` (stables), `pool:USDC/cbBTC/500` (btc), `pool:WETH/USDC/3000` (risk); `twapWindow` is
`conservador-live-v2`'s own `execution.twapWindowSeconds` (1,800 s), matching spec 007's proposed `twapWindow`.

Three independent measurements, computed from the same dataset and the same backtest run per window (deposit
1,000 and 100,000 USDC, `conservador-live-v2`, sessions enforced and renewed — the real, deployed engine
behaviour, not a hypothetical v4 run):

1. **Band refusals** (`scanBand`/`bandGrid`, a pure scan of `|spot - 30-min TWAP|` against the dataset's own
   ticks and oracle cumulatives — no engine, no run): how often and for how long spot sits more than
   `band x factor` ticks from the TWAP, per pool, for bands {5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200} and factors {0.7, 0.8, 0.9,
   1.0}. Factor 1.0 is the deployed, un-narrowed band (the real on-chain refusal threshold); 0.7-0.9 bracket the
   engine's own conservative narrowing (`@mamoru/uniswap-v3/policy-bounds`'s `DEFAULT_BAND_FACTOR_BPS = 0.8`).
2. **Exit waits** (`exitWaits`): of the engine's real, executed `rerange`/`reduce` ops in that same run (both
   close a position's liquidity through `decreaseLiquidity`; spec 007 "(a) price" notes the minimums at the band
   edges keep a well-inside-band decrease safe, but a spot past an edge with a range that extends past it fails
   the edge minimum at execution — that is the failure mode measured here), how many land while the band would
   refuse them and for how long until the TWAP catches up, plus the pool's own price move over the wait as a
   directional cost proxy.
3. **Budgets** (`scanBudget`): `W_p`/`T_p` resolved exactly as the v4 grants resolve them
   (`v4CapFormulas`/`computeCaps`, `@mamoru/policy`, at this run's activation prices), against the real token
   spend of the run's swaps and mints (`OpEntry.spend`, this unit's own addition to `run.ts`; a decrease spends
   nothing against the budget, spec 007 "(c) budgets"). One Lab window is treated as one approval activated at its
   own start — the windows are disjoint historical excerpts, not one continuous timeline, so a total is never
   summed across windows (see the budgets table's own note).

**Sampling bias, stated up front**: `LAB_WINDOWS` deliberately over-represents stress (6 of 13 are crash weeks, one
a rally, one a stable-pair stress week; only 4 are calm months, one drawn at random each). The breach rates below
describe the Lab's history, not an ordinary unweighted year — they are closer to "how bad does it get in a stress
week" than "how often does this happen in a random week."

## Data gaps

`pool:USDC/cbBTC/500` has **zero** samples in `aug2024` (`base-2024-08-crash-10m.json` lists the pool but every
sample is null — `windows.ts`'s own note: "no cbBTC on Base yet"). The band scan still ran for the other two pools
on that window; the engine run (a 3-bucket account needs all three bucket pools to decide at all) could not run for
`aug2024` and is excluded from the exit-wait and budget tables. No other window or pool is missing data (checked:
all three pools read at every sample of the other 12 windows).

## 1. Band refusals

Aggregated over the 12 (13 for stables/risk) runnable windows. Full grid: `results/raw/t5-tables.md` and
`results/raw/t5-raw.json` (`bandRows`, episodes summarized to count/longest/total — rerun for full per-episode
detail).

At the **proposed** bands, factor 1.0 (the deployed threshold):

| Pool | Band | Samples | Breach samples | Breach rate | Episodes | Longest episode | Total breach time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stables (USDC/USDT) | 10 | 35,139 | 42 | 0.12% | 21 | 70 min | 3.3 h |
| btc (USDC/cbBTC) | 100 | 34,131 | 126 | 0.37% | 83 | 50 min | 6.4 h |
| risk (WETH/USDC) | 100 | 35,139 | 469 | 1.33% | 293 | 90 min | 27.6 h |

At the engine's own narrower factor (0.8, `DEFAULT_BAND_FACTOR_BPS`), the same bands breach a bit more often
(stables 0.14%/50 samples, btc 0.73%/250, risk 2.23%/785) — expected, since 0.8x of a band is a tighter gate than
the band itself; this is the engine being conservative in what it submits, not a new refusal mode.

Two things worth the owner's attention:

- **Risk breaches almost 4x more than btc at the same 100-tick band** (1.33% vs 0.37% of samples, 27.6 h vs 6.4 h
  total over the same stretch of history). A tick is the same ~0.01% price step in both pools (ticks are
  fee-tier-independent), so this is a real difference in how far WETH/USDC's spot wanders from its own 30-minute
  TWAP, not a unit artifact. The proposed table gives both pools the same band "= `maxTwapDeviationTicks`,
  the engine's own #46 minimum (50 bp) is tighter" — true for btc, and also true for risk (same #46 rule), but the
  margin is thinner for risk.
- **Stables is rare but not zero**: band 10 is breached in 21 separate episodes over the measured history, longest
  70 minutes. The grid shows halving the breach count needs very little room (band 20 already drops total breach
  time to 1.5 h, band 50 to under a minute) — see the exit-wait sensitivity below for what that buys against what
  it costs.

## 2. Exit waits (rerange/reduce)

Full table: `results/raw/t5-tables.md`. At the **deployed** band (factor 1.0), summed over both deposits:

| Pool | Band | Real exits | Ever refused | Median wait | p95 wait | Max wait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stables | 10 | 39 | 14 (36%) | 10 min | 20-80 min | 1.3 h |
| btc | 100 | 28 | 0 (0%) | - | - | - |
| risk | 100 | 133 | 0 (0%) | - | - | - |

**btc and risk: zero of the engine's real historical exits were ever refused by their proposed, deployed
100-tick band**, across every crash, rally and calm window in the Lab's set, at either deposit. The band-refusal
scan above shows the market does cross 100 ticks sometimes (0.37%/1.33% of samples), but none of those crossings
happened to land on a sample where the engine had also just decided to reduce or re-range that pool's position —
exits are not random-time draws, and in this history they did not coincide with the worst spot/TWAP gaps. This is
the strongest evidence in this report **for** the proposed btc/risk bands: no observed liveness cost at the
deployed threshold, only at the engine's own narrower (0.8x) submission threshold, where a handful did wait
(btc: 3 of 15 / 2 of 13; risk: 16 of 70 / 14 of 63), always clearing within 30 minutes.

**Stables is the opposite case.** At the deployed 10-tick band, 10 of 23 exits (43%) at 1,000 USDC and 4 of 16
(25%) at 100,000 USDC were refused at the moment they were proposed, waiting a median 10 minutes but up to 1.3
hours, with the pool's own price moving up to -73 bp away from parity during the wait (direction: token0 of this
pool is USDC, so a negative move here is USDC depegging down or USDT firming — either way, adverse for a
stables-pool exit that is waiting). Widening the band lowers this quickly but does not zero it out in this
history:

| Band | Refused (of 23, 1,000 USDC) | Refused (of 16, 100,000 USDC) | Worst price move during the wait |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 (proposed) | 10 (43%) | 4 (25%) | -72.73 bp |
| 20 | 4 (17%) | 1 (6%) | -48.88 bp |
| 30 | 3 (13%) | 1 (6%) | -69.75 bp |
| 40 | 1 (4%) | 0 (0%) | -91.57 bp |

Widening does not monotonically shrink the worst case (band 40's one remaining episode is worse than band 20's or
30's) — fewer, rarer breaches select for the tail events, not the median ones. Spec 007's own max-loss bound is
linear in band, so this is a genuine trade: every tick added to the stables band to cut liveness cost also raises
the manipulation bound that pool is exposed to by the same tick. Given the pool's small share of a leaked key's
loss (spec 007 "Max loss": stables is 0.11% x notional vs btc's 1.05% and risk's 1.3%), this report's reading is
that **10 ticks is still defensible** — the absolute bps at stake from widening are small everywhere on this
table — but it is a real, measured trade-off for Ot's sign-off (spec 007 open question #3), not a free win.

Direction notes for `priceMoveBps` (token0 of each pool, per `V4_POOLS`): stables and btc both have USDC as
token0, so a negative move is USDC/stable-side weakening; risk has WETH as token0, so a negative move is the
ordinary "ETH fell further" reading. For btc the **adverse** direction for an exit is therefore positive (USDC
strengthening = cbBTC falling further): at the engine's 0.8x band, the two btc episodes that did wait saw a
further cbBTC fall of 22 bp and 759 bp respectively during the wait — a real, if rare, cost, even though the
deployed (non-narrowed) band was never once the binding constraint in this history.

## 3. Budgets: W_p (1xCp per day) and T_p (3xCp per approval)

Full table: `results/raw/t5-tables.md`. "Cp" is each pool's share of the account cap (stables 50%, btc 40%, risk
10%, `conservador-live-v2`'s own bucket weights, reused by `V4_POOLS.capShareBps`). Figures below are at 1,000
USDC deposit (100,000 scales proportionally and shows the same pattern — see the raw table).

| Pool | Token | W_p | Peak day spend | Peak vs W_p | Days over W_p | T_p | Worst run vs T_p | Runs over T_p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stables | USDC | 500 | 1,837.86 | 3.68x | 7 of 210 | 1,500 | 155% | 3 of 12 |
| stables | USDT | 500.61 | 2,429.63 | 4.85x | 4 of 210 | 1,501.82 | 178% | 2 of 12 |
| btc | USDC | 400 | 593.57 | 1.48x | 5 of 210 | 1,200 | 95% | 0 of 12 |
| btc | cbBTC | 0.005178 | 0.010039 | 1.94x | 3 of 210 | 0.015533 | 99% | 0 of 12 |
| risk | WETH | 0.041483 | 0.098570 | 2.38x | 8 of 210 | 0.124448 | 177% | 5 of 12 |
| risk | USDC | 100 | 201.45 | 2.01x | 12 of 210 | 300 | 161% | 5 of 12 |

**This is the finding this unit changes the sign on: both W_p and T_p, as proposed, would regularly have blocked
the engine's real turnover, not just in a crash tail.**

- **W_p (1xCp/day)** is exceeded on 2-12 of the 210 pool-days sampled across the Lab's windows (1-6%, concentrated
  in volatile weeks but not confined to the most extreme ones), by 1.5x to 4.85x on the day it happens. The risk
  pool's USDC leg is the most frequent offender (12 of 210 days, 5.7%).
- **T_p (3xCp/approval)** is already exceeded by the engine's real turnover *within a single Lab window* — at
  most a month, several only a week — for every pool except btc (which comes within 1-5% of its own cap without
  crossing it). `conservador-live-v2`'s session lasts a full year (`session.validitySeconds = 365 * 86_400`,
  "one approval lasts a year", `packages/policy/src/policies.ts`); if turnover over a full year is even roughly
  proportional to turnover over the Lab's sampled days (a generous assumption for the calmer months, which are
  under-represented here), `T_p = 3xCp` would be exhausted in the opening weeks of essentially every approval, for
  stables and risk at least. That is not a rare tail cost, it is the default outcome at the stated approval length.
- btc is the one pool close to fitting as proposed: its W_p peaks are the smallest multiple (1.48x/1.94x) and its
  T_p was never exceeded in any of the 12 runs (worst 95-99%, i.e. close but inside).

**This is independent of the band finding above** (spec 007 is explicit the two are separate checks, and this Lab
run used the deployed `conservador-live-v2` turnover, not a version throttled by either). A refusal from either
W_p or T_p has the same operational shape as a band refusal — the engine waits — so the "Liveness cost" framing of
§2 extends here: if W_p/T_p bind as proposed, the engine would wait on ordinary rebalancing turnover, not only
during a manipulation attempt, several times a month per pool.

**What this means for the max-loss bound** (spec 007 "Max loss with a leaked key", `L <= sum_p (band_p + fee_p) x
(V_p + 2 x min(T_p, W_p x windows until revoked))`): loosening T_p to stop blocking ordinary turnover raises this
bound directly and linearly. The spec's own worked example already uses `T_p = 3C` to get 4.3% of the account
cap "never revoked" — a T_p sized to the turnover actually measured here (see the recommendation below) raises
that figure proportionally. This is the real trade the data surfaces: a budget tight enough to bound the loss of a
leaked key as tightly as spec 007's worked example is also tight enough to regularly stall the legitimate engine.

## 4. Recommendation

| Pool | Band (ticks) | Factor | W_p | T_p | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stables (USDC/USDT 0.01%) | 10 (as proposed) | 0.8 | >= 5xCp/day | needs a longer-than-one-Lab-window measurement; see below | Band: 0.12% breach rate, rare, bounded cost; widening trades liveness for a wider loss bound without zeroing the tail (table above). Budget: peak day spend reached 3.68-4.85x Cp — W_p at 1x would bind 4-7 of 210 sampled days. |
| btc (USDC/cbBTC 0.05%) | 100 (as proposed) | 0.8 | ~2xCp/day | ~1xCp/day headroom at 3xCp/approval; close to fitting as proposed | Band: zero of 28 real historical exits ever refused at the deployed band. Budget: the one pool whose measured turnover is close to the proposed sizing (peak day 1.48-1.94x, T_p never breached, 95-99% of cap in the worst run). |
| risk (WETH/USDC 0.3%) | 100 (as proposed, flag for a second look) | 0.8 | >= 3xCp/day | needs a longer-than-one-Lab-window measurement; see below | Band: zero of 133 real historical exits refused at the deployed band (good), but the pool breaches the band 3.6x more often than btc at the same threshold (1.33% vs 0.37% of samples) — worth a dedicated look before final sign-off, even though it did not bind any exit here. Budget: the most frequently over-budget pool (12 of 210 days over W_p on its USDC leg; T_p already exceeded in 5 of 12 runs). |

**Bands**: keep 10/100/100 as proposed. The strongest evidence either way is that the engine's real exits were
never refused by the deployed (un-narrowed) btc or risk bands in 2+ years of Lab history across every regime,
and the stables band's cost, while real, is small in absolute terms. The one open item is risk's higher raw
breach rate relative to btc at the same band — not disqualifying (it never cost a real exit here), but the
margin is thinner than btc's, worth a note in T6's audit scope.

**Budgets**: W_p = 1xCp/day and T_p = 3xCp/approval, as proposed, are both too tight against this Lab's measured
turnover and need to move before T7. This report gives per-pool peak-day multiples (1.48x-4.85x) as a floor for
resizing W_p; a budget set to, say, 5-6xCp/day would clear every peak day measured here with some margin. T_p is
the harder number: a 3x multiple is sized as if the budget window and the approval length were close together,
but the approval here is a year and the budget window is a day — 3xCp buys roughly 3 days of headroom against a
365-day approval, which this report shows is not enough even against a single volatile week. Fixing this is a
policy-sizing decision for Ot and T2/T7, not something this report decides unilaterally; two directions, both
consistent with the measurements above:
1. Size T_p against the approval length instead of a fixed small multiple of W_p (e.g. a cap that assumes
   something closer to `W_p` worth of real spend on most days of the approval, not 3 days' worth over a year), or
2. Keep T_p small but renew far more often than once a year in practice (the #149 usage-triggered early renewal
   already exists for the UniActionPolicy caps; the same owner-tap renewal would need to fire for
   `MamoruActionPolicy`'s own budgets before they bind, not just once validitySeconds lapses).
Either way, accepting 3xCp per approval at today's one-year validity, unchanged, means the engine should expect to
wait on its own budget within the first few weeks of most approvals, for every pool except btc.
