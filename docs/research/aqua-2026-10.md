# 1inch Aqua on Base: experimental Lab slot (otto/mamoru#183)

Research + fork probe for an experimental Aqua slot in the Lab, next to the Uniswap v3 slot we run today.
Probe: [`packages/scenarios/aqua/probe.ts`](../../packages/scenarios/aqua/probe.ts). All numbers below are from a
real run of that probe against a Base fork (finalized block 52767735, anvil, upstream RPC never printed). Nothing
was sent to a real chain.

## 2. Mechanics, confirmed by running them

- **Allowance, not custody.** `ship()`/`dock()` move no tokens; both are pure accounting. Verified: a 10,000 USDC +
  5 WETH Safe's real wallet balance was byte-for-byte unchanged immediately before and after `ship()` (158,945 gas
  owner-signed approval; 153,472 gas `ship()`), while Aqua's own `rawBalances()` counter went from 0 to
  7,999,999,999 (raw USDC units) for that `(maker, app, strategyHash, token)` key.
- **The maker is `msg.sender`, not a parameter.** Neither `ship(address app, bytes strategy, address[] tokens,
  uint256[] amounts)` nor `dock(address app, bytes32 strategyHash, address[] tokens)` takes a maker argument — the
  `Order`'s own `maker` field is metadata for the strategy's identity/hash, not what Aqua's registry keys its
  storage on. **For the Safe to be the maker, `ship()`/`dock()` must be sent as Safe transactions** (this probe
  signs them with the passkey, via the same `ownerExec` path the product uses for owner actions), never as a plain
  relayer call. Getting this wrong is a silent failure mode: the transaction succeeds, gas is spent, and the
  strategy is shipped — just under the relayer's address as maker, invisible to the Safe. First attempt at this
  probe hit exactly that bug; every subsequent `quote()`/`swap()` against the "shipped" strategy reverted with
  `SafeBalancesForTokenNotInActiveStrategy(address,address,bytes32,address)` until the fix.
- **Template chosen: concentrated (`AquaXYCAmmStrategy.newConcentrate`).** Of the documented templates (`xyc`
  constant-product, `concentrated`, `pegged`), concentrated is the one comparable to the Uniswap v3 position the
  Lab runs today. Built from `sqrtPriceMin`/`sqrtPriceMax` (1e18 fixed-point, `P = tokenGt/tokenLt` by numeric
  address, no decimals normalization — see "SDK gotchas"), with `.withFeeTokenIn(bps)` for the maker's fee.
- **A fill, measured.** A taker swapped 500 USDC into the shipped strategy (fee 5 bps, matching the Uniswap
  `pool:WETH/USDC/500` fee tier used as the baseline below) and received 205,700,593,251,626,013 wei WETH
  (157,464 gas). An identically sized control strategy (same price band, same liquidity `L`, fee 0 bps) quoted
  205,802,855,857,836,719 wei WETH for the same 500 USDC in the same block — a 4 bps difference, consistent with
  the 5 bps fee (the gap to the nominal 5 bps is price-impact rounding inside the curve, not a bug).
- **Fee earned by the maker.** `rawBalances()` is the maker's aggregate virtual reserve, not a line-itemed fee
  balance, so reading it alone cannot prove a fee was charged: a 500 USDC push looks identical whether `feeBps` is
  5 or 0. What actually demonstrates the fee is the A/B: reading `rawBalances()` immediately before and after the
  fill showed the maker's virtual USDC balance moved by exactly 500,000,000 — the taker's full gross input, no
  premium credited there — which only establishes that the fee is *not* an input-side effect. The fee shows up on
  the output side instead: the identically sized, fee-free control strategy's quote (same `L`, same price band,
  same block) would have delivered 205,802,855,857,836,719 wei WETH against the 205,700,593,251,626,013 actually
  delivered, a 102,262,606,210,706 wei WETH (4 bps) shortfall against a strategy set to 5 bps. The probe only
  counts the fee as confirmed when this output-side shortfall is positive and within the configured `feeBps` — a
  misencoded or missing fee instruction would surface as a failed check here, not be masked by an unconditional
  pass. That shortfall, at the fork's spot price, is the maker's real, kept value on this one fill — not the
  nominal `feeBps * amountIn` arithmetic alone. The fee stays entirely with the maker unless the strategy also sets
  `withProtocolFee(bps, receiver)` (an opt-in field for an app/referrer split; this strategy did not set it).
- **Overcommit, reproduced.** After the fill, the owner moved the Safe's entire real WETH balance to a dead address
  (96,990 gas) **without docking first**. Aqua's virtual balance for the strategy still showed the old WETH amount —
  nothing in `ship()`/`dock()` re-checks real balance. The next taker fill against the same strategy reverted with
  `SafeTransferFromFailed()`, the router's own `pull()` failing because the maker's wallet no longer holds the
  tokens Aqua still thinks are shippable. This is the overcommit failure mode the design note flagged as a risk,
  reproduced on a real fork rather than assumed from the docs. It is not limited to "shipping more than the
  allowance": spending the same real tokens through an unrelated protocol (a v3 mint, an ordinary transfer) after
  shipping is just as effective at creating it, because Aqua's bookkeeping is never re-validated against the real
  balance until a fill tries to move it.
- **Rebalance = dock + ship, no tokens move either step.** Docked the first strategy and shipped a new one at a
  shifted price band (the old max became the new min, simulating the price having moved up): 104,709 + 153,412 =
  258,121 gas as two separate Safe transactions, or **177,594 gas** batched into one Safe transaction (as production
  would actually send it, via `multiSendCallOnly`, signed by the passkey alone).

## 3. Gas per rebalance: Aqua vs the Uniswap v3 cycle, same fork, same Safe

The v3 side is not an ad-hoc cycle invented for this comparison: it is the exact action set of this repo's own
`manage-any` session grant for re-ranging a position (`packages/policy/src/grants.ts`) —
`decreaseLiquidity` → `collect` → `burn` → `approve` (token0) → `approve` (token1) → `mint`. Burning the emptied
NFT and re-approving both tokens per cycle are both things the product already does on every re-range, not gas
inflators added to make Aqua look better; omitting them would have understated the v3 side.

| Path | Gas (1 batched Safe tx) |
|---|---|
| Aqua: `dock()` + `ship()` at a shifted range | **177,594** |
| Uniswap v3: `decreaseLiquidity` + `collect` + `burn` + `approve`×2 + `mint` | 591,552 |

Aqua's rebalance used **~30% of the gas** of the equivalent Uniswap v3 cycle on the same fork, same Safe, same
passkey signer, same block range of prices — a **~3.3x** gas reduction (about 70% less gas). This is the single
most attractive number for the Lab slot: no token movement means no `transferFrom`, no pool tick-crossing
accounting, no NFT state writes, just two registry bookkeeping calls. It does not capture LVR, fee APR, or
capacity — those need live data, not a fork probe (see "Metrics" below).

The two-separate-transactions figure (258,121 gas) is included because a real session/userOp flow might not always
batch; even unbatched, Aqua's rebalance is still cheaper than the v3 cycle. Note the v3 side's gas did **not** grow
linearly when `burn()` was added (591,552 with `burn()` vs 636,066 measured without it in an earlier pass of this
probe): clearing the position's storage slot earns an EVM storage refund, which can more than offset the added
call's own cost. The ~3.3x figure already includes that effect; it is not a rounding error.

## 4. Session-key feasibility: can `UniActionPolicy` scope `ship()`/`dock()`?

`UniActionPolicy` (the policy this repo already uses for `enter-swap`/`enter-mint`/`manage` in
`packages/policy/src/grants.ts`) compares one 32-byte calldata word at a fixed `index*32` offset after the
selector, up to 16 rules per `(target, selector)` pair, with `EQUAL`/`GT`/`LT`/`GTE`/`LTE`/`NOT_EQUAL`/`IN_RANGE`. It
has no way to iterate an array or decode a dynamic blob — a rule can only pin one word.

**`ship(address app, bytes strategy, address[] tokens, uint256[] amounts)`** — calldata captured from the real
`shipTx` this probe sent (selector `0xf50b870f`):

| Word | Content | Pinnable? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `app` (static address) | **Yes** — `EQUAL AquaSwapVMRouter` |
| 1 | offset to `strategy` | No — always `0x80` regardless of strategy content |
| 2 | offset to `tokens` | No — varies with strategy length, points into the tail |
| 3 | offset to `amounts` | No — same |

Only word 0 is a real constraint. The strategy bytes (price range, fee, salt — everything that determines what the
Safe is actually committing), the token list and the amounts are all in the dynamic tail, unreachable by a
fixed-offset rule. This is the same shape problem the routing design note already flagged for Uniswap v4's
`modifyLiquidities`.

**`dock(address app, bytes32 strategyHash, address[] tokens)`** — calldata from the real `dockTx` (selector
`0x28defc17`):

| Word | Content | Pinnable? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `app` (static address) | **Yes** — `EQUAL AquaSwapVMRouter` |
| 1 | `strategyHash` (static `bytes32`) | **Yes** — `EQUAL <one pre-shipped hash>` |
| 2 | offset to `tokens` | No — always `0x60` (vacuous: there is only one dynamic field), but `AquaApp` already reverts `DockingShouldCloseAllTokens` unless the full token list is given, so this word needs no policy help |

**Answer:** `dock()` can be scoped tightly today — `app = AquaSwapVMRouter`, `strategyHash = <one strategy>` — the
same shape as this repo's existing `manage` grant pinning one `tokenId`. A session key could safely be granted
"retire this one pre-shipped strategy." `ship()` cannot be meaningfully scoped past the app address with
`UniActionPolicy` alone: entering a new range (or rebalancing, which needs a fresh `ship()`) requires either an
owner-signed step — what this probe does, passkey `ownerExec`, the same path the product already uses for owner
actions — or a purpose-built executor contract with fixed-offset arguments, the same unresolved option the design
note already names for Uniswap v4.

## 5. Risks

- **Overcommit.** Reproduced above: virtual balance is never re-checked against real balance until a fill tries to
  move it. A fill against an insolvent strategy reverts cleanly (no funds lost on that fill), but the maker's
  strategy is silently broken until docked and re-shipped, and **any** spend of the real tokens elsewhere — not
  just another `ship()` — can trigger it.
- **Allowance surface.** The Safe's allowance to `Aqua.sol` is the actual ceiling on loss, not the strategy's
  stated size. A revoked or reduced allowance does not retroactively shrink an already-shipped virtual balance; it
  only caps what a *future* `ship()` could add. The allowance itself is the number that matters for "how much is at
  risk," not whatever the currently-active strategy claims.
- **Strategy immutability.** No in-place parameter change, ever — "rebalancing" is always dock + ship (confirmed:
  this probe's shifted-range strategy is a different `strategyHash`, not an update to the old one). That is a
  feature for gas (cheap to redo) but means every parameter change is a brand-new, unaudited-by-anyone-but-the-maker
  position: there is no "edit" path to review before it goes live.
- **Fee economics are not guaranteed.** The docs are explicit: "swap fees are not guaranteed and do not offset
  impermanent loss in every market." The fee is a maker-set parameter (`withFeeTokenIn`), not a protocol floor —
  Aqua applies no protocol-level take by default (an opt-in `withProtocolFee` field exists for an app/referrer
  split, unused by this probe).
- **Maturity/audits.** Launched mid-2026. Eight firms audited the two releases checked
  (Bailsec, Decurity, MixBytes, OpenZeppelin, Theori for v1.0.2; those five plus Hashlock, Hexens, Nethermind for
  v1) per the [1inch-audits repo](https://github.com/1inch/1inch-audits) index — the reports themselves were not
  read in this pass. The protocol's own messaging ("audits reduce risk, they do not remove it: the code is audited,
  but new") is a fair summary for a low-cap experimental slot.
- **SDK/dependency risk, not protocol risk, but real for us.** See below.

### SDK gotchas (found running this, not in any doc)

- `@1inch/aqua-sdk@0.3.4` depends on `@1inch/sdk-core@0.1.5`; `@1inch/swap-vm-sdk@0.4.4` (latest at time of writing)
  depends on `@1inch/sdk-core@0.1.6`. Bun installs both copies side by side, and TypeScript treats their
  `Address`/`HexString` classes as distinct nominal types (private field declarations differ by file), so mixing
  values across the two SDKs — the normal way to use them together, per their own quick-start examples — does not
  typecheck. Fixed here by pinning `@1inch/swap-vm-sdk` to `0.4.3`, the release that shares `sdk-core@0.1.5` with
  `aqua-sdk@0.3.4`. Anyone upgrading either package needs to re-check this pairing.
- `@1inch/swap-vm-sdk`'s own README worked example for `AquaXYCAmmStrategy.newConcentrate` (`rawPriceMin =
  1e18/3000` for a USDC/WETH range) skips decimal normalization between the two tokens. Verified against
  `1inch/swap-vm`'s `XYCConcentrate.sol` (v1.0.2 tag): `P = tokenGt/tokenLt` is a **pure raw-unit ratio** — the
  caller must account for the 12-decimal gap between USDC (6) and WETH (18) themselves. The README's shortcut is
  off by `10^12` for exactly this pair. This probe derives the price band from the live Uniswap v3 pool's
  `sqrtPriceX96` instead (which already encodes the same raw-unit convention, decimals included), and uses
  `sqrtPriceMin`/`sqrtPriceMax` rather than `rawPriceMin`/`rawPriceMax` to avoid re-deriving it by hand.

## 6. Recommendation for the Lab

Keep the slot, with the design note's "low cap, experimental" framing and these additions:

1. **Ship/dock only as Safe transactions, never a session key, until a dock-only grant exists.** The maker-is-
   `msg.sender` behavior makes this a correctness requirement, not just a security preference.
2. **A `dock`-only session grant is implementable now** (`app EQUAL`, `strategyHash EQUAL`), mirroring `manage`.
   `ship` stays owner-signed (or behind a future executor) until there is a reason to automate entry.
3. **Cap the allowance, not just the strategy size**, and treat the allowance as the loss ceiling in any risk
   write-up — it is the number overcommit actually bounds.
4. **Track the gas win as the headline, not the APR.** 177,594 vs 591,552 gas per rebalance (same fork, same Safe)
   is real and reproducible; fee APR is not measurable from a fork and needs live Base data once the slot runs.

## Metrics the Lab should track once this runs live

Per the design note (§4) and the venues research note's LVR framing (Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden, Zhang 2022;
[arXiv:2305.14604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14604)):

- **Fee APR net of LVR.** Gross fee income (5 bps per fill observed here, maker-set, adjustable) minus the
  predictable LVR cost of running a concentrated range, the same way the v3 slot is already reported — raw fee APR
  is not comparable across venues without it.
- **Gas and rebalance cost.** This probe's 177,594 (Aqua) vs 591,552 (v3) gas per rebalance, re-measured against
  live gas prices periodically — Base gas prices move, the ratio should hold but the absolute ETH cost won't.
- **Time in range / rebalance frequency.** How often the price leaves the shipped band, since every re-range is a
  brand-new strategy (immutability), not a cheap in-place adjustment of ticks.
- **Capacity.** How much one strategy can absorb before the maker's allowance (not the strategy's stated size)
  becomes the binding constraint, and how fills against a large strategy move its effective price (curve depth),
  not just whether the fill succeeds.
- **Incentive dependence.** Whether any observed fee income depends on 1inch-side routing/incentives rather than
  organic taker flow, since Aqua has no public aggregator volume history yet to check against.
